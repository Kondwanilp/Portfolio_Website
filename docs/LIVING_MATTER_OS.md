### 11. CONTINUOUS SCROLL-RESPONSIVE MOTION SYSTEM

Implement a shared adaptive animation system that combines autonomous playback with scroll-responsive acceleration.

The animations must not depend entirely on scrolling.

#### Expected behavior

When a visitor enters an animated section and remains stationary:

- The animation plays naturally at its intended cinematic speed.

When the visitor begins scrolling:

- The animation responds to the magnitude of their scrolling velocity.

When the visitor scrolls slowly:

- The animation accelerates gently.

When the visitor scrolls quickly:

- The animation accelerates more significantly.

When the visitor stops scrolling:

- Playback smoothly returns to its natural rate.
- The animation continues from its existing progress.

When the visitor scrolls completely past the animated section:

- Gracefully finalize the animation and reveal the appropriate content.

When the visitor returns to the section:

- Allow the animation to replay naturally when appropriate.

Do not reset animations repeatedly when someone makes minor adjustments near a viewport boundary.

#### Technical expectations

Create a reusable motion controller that supports:

- Normalized animation progress.
- Smoothed scroll velocity.
- Adaptive playback rates.
- Clamped acceleration.
- Smooth interpolation.
- Natural autonomous playback.
- Viewport detection.
- Animation lifecycle management.
- Re-entry thresholds.
- Cancellation and cleanup.
- Reduced-motion preferences.
- Mobile touch scrolling.
- Graceful fallbacks.

Use acceleration limits that preserve recognizable motion.

Consider a normal playback rate of 1×, with adaptive acceleration up to approximately 3× or 4× when appropriate.

Adjust these values through actual testing.

Do not introduce sudden speed changes, reversed playback, flickering, unwanted reinitialization, or visible jumps.

Never scrolljack.

Never lock the user's scrolling.

Never artificially delay navigation.

The visitor's behavior should influence the animation, but the animation must never control the visitor.
